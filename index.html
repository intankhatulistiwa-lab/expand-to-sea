The Hard Truth

Why Indonesia
acquisition fails

Foreign B2B tech companies enter Indonesia with hope. Most leave with burned budget. These are the patterns I see over and over.

01
No bridge between HQ playbook and Indonesian reality

What works in Singapore, the US, or Europe rarely works here. Trust signals, buying behavior, and channel dynamics are fundamentally different. Copying the HQ playbook without translation burns budget and misses the market.

02
Awareness is not pipeline

Events, PR, brand campaigns — they create visibility but not SQLs. If no one defined what a qualified lead is and how to generate them consistently, awareness just becomes expensive noise.

03
Long B2B sales cycles burn your runway

B2B deals in Indonesia take time — longer decision cycles, more stakeholders, slower trust-building. If acquisition isn't optimized to shorten the cycle, runway disappears before pipeline converts.

Past Projects

What I've fixed.

Anonymized case studies from my time inside Indonesian startups.

Enterprise

Focus replaced breadth. SQLs increased 4x.

Challenge
The company was running too many acquisition channels at once. Effort was spread thin, and every channel was producing only minimal leads.
Approach
Diagnosed each active channel — what it produced, what it cost, whether it had real SQL potential. Cut the ones that weren't pulling weight. Doubled down on the ones that were.
Outcome
Sales Qualified Leads increased 4x.
Micro Business

Cutting acquisition cost from $25 to below $1.

Challenge
Every new user went through a heavy in-house process — find, convince, educate, train. Cost per acquisition sat around $25, and activation took two weeks.
Approach
Instead of scaling the sales team, identified local partners already trusted by micro businesses — they knew where the businesses were, already had trust, and could handle training.
Outcome
Cost per acquired user dropped below $1. Activation time: 3 days max.
Small and Medium Business

Reduced churn from above 30% to below 15%.

Challenge
No retention process existed. Customers only heard from the company near subscription end. Churn was above 30%, and the team had no visibility into why customers stayed or left.
Approach
Used product usage data to identify retention patterns. Segmented customers based on those patterns. Rebuilt lifecycle communication with triggers at every sensitive touchpoint.
Outcome
Churn reduced from above 30% to below 15%.
